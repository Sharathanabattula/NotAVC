## How to use this file

This is a working document, not a post. It becomes `/thesis` on the site only
when the positions below are ones you could defend in a room with someone who
does this for a living.

**The rule for every line in here: it must be falsifiable.** "Fintech is
exciting" is not a thesis. "Cost of funds, not app quality, decides who
survives the next RBI tightening — and I'd expect the gap between bank-backed
and standalone NBFC lenders to widen by X bps within 18 months" is a thesis,
because it can be wrong and you'd know when.

**The differentiator: this thesis shows its revisions.** Funds publish
polished conviction because admitting error costs them LP money. You have no
LPs. Every `v1 → v2` with an honest "here's what I got wrong" is something a
partner structurally cannot publish — and it is the same correction layer
that runs the rest of the brand.

## 1. The lane

**Indian fintech, viewed through regulation.**

Why this one and not consumer unit economics:
- Fewer people can do it well. Reading an RBI circular is a barrier;
  reading a DRHP is not.
- Regulation is the only durable moat left in Indian fintech — product and
  UI are copied in a quarter, a licence is not.
- The evidence is public but unglamorous: RBI circulars, NBFC filings,
  master directions. Nobody is fighting you for it.

The cost, stated honestly: the data is thinner than consumer. You will
sometimes have a view you cannot fully evidence. Say so when that happens
rather than dressing an opinion as analysis.

## Research spine

The thesis is only as good as what feeds it. Sources worth a standing habit:

| Source | What it gives you | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| RBI **master directions** and circulars | The actual rules, before the coverage | Weekly scan |
| RBI **Financial Stability Report** | Systemic view, NPA trends, sector stress | Half-yearly |
| **NBFC annual reports** (filed with MCA) | Cost of funds, NIM, credit costs | Per company |
| Company **DRHPs** | The most honest document a startup ever files | On filing |
| **Ken / Morning Context** | Reporting that reads filings, not press releases | Ongoing |

**The one-hour rule** (from the Manus community blueprint): one raw filing a
day, read properly. Ten hours of filings beats a hundred hours of newsletters,
and it is the only way to build a view nobody else has.

## The starter questions

Answer these in your own words and the four positions above will mostly write
themselves. Don't research first — write what you already suspect, then go
find out whether you were right. The gap between the two is the content.

1. **What does everyone believe about Indian fintech that you think is wrong?**
   Not a nuance — something you'd actually argue about.

2. **When the RBI tightened unsecured lending, who did you expect to die, and
   who actually did?** Where were you wrong?

3. **Is a lending licence a moat or a leash?** Pick a side.

4. **Name a fintech you would not invest in at any price.** Why — structurally,
   not because you dislike the product.

5. **What would have to be true for a standalone Indian fintech to beat a
   bank-backed one over ten years?** If you can't construct that case, that
   itself is a position.
