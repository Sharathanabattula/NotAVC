"Make in India" still begins with "ship to India."

That single line explains our semiconductor industry — or the lack of one.

India has roughly 20% of the world's chip-design engineers. Yet almost every physical input that turns a design into a chip — wafers, lithography tools, ultrapure gases, photoresists, masks — is imported.

How did we get here? Compare the playbooks:

→ Taiwan (1970s): named semiconductors a national priority, recruited Morris Chang from the US, gave him the capital, and founded TSMC in 1987. One agenda, one owner.

→ India: the push was spread across multiple ministries, each moving at its own pace. No single throat to choke.

That's slowly changing:

• Semicon 2.0 — a $13.3B (₹1.275 trillion) package approved July 15, 2026, funding the whole chain: design, equipment, materials, fabs, packaging, R&D and talent. The first push chased fabs; this one funds the tools and materials we still import.

• On the ground — Tata and PSMC are building India's first commercial fab in Dholera, Gujarat: an ~$11B facility targeting 28nm, ~50,000 wafers/month.

The honest caveat: it's a start, not the finish line. The first fab is only slated to be commissioned in 2028, launch output is weighted to mature nodes, and equipment + materials remain almost entirely imported. Money committed ≠ a delivered supply chain.

But if Semicon 2.0 delivers, "Make in India" might finally stop beginning with "ship to India."

Sources: India Semiconductor Mission / Semicon 2.0 cabinet approval; Tata Electronics × PSMC announcements. Figures theirs, framing mine.
