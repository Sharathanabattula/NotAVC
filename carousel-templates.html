1. Term sheet
A non-binding agreement that outlines the key terms of an investment — valuation, equity stake, board seats, liquidation preferences.

Think of it like a rental agreement for your company. Except the landlord might get to kick you out if things go wrong.

80% of term sheet terms are negotiable. Most first-time founders don't know this.

2. Cap table
A spreadsheet showing who owns what percentage of the company — founders, investors, ESOP pool.

Every funding round dilutes existing shareholders. If you don't track it, you wake up owning 8% of the company you built.
Pro tip: Always model your cap table 3 rounds ahead. What looks like 20% today could be 6% after Series C.

4. Liquidation preference
Determines who gets paid first when a company is sold or liquidated.

A 1x liquidation preference means the investor gets their money back before anyone else. A 2x means they get double.

This is why a $100M exit can leave founders with nothing if the preferences stack up.

5. Runway
How many months a startup can survive at its current burn rate before running out of cash.

Runway = Cash in bank ÷ Monthly burn

Most VCs want to see 18-24 months of runway after investing. Anything less means you'll be fundraising again before you've proven anything.
Indian startups in 2024 had a median runway of just 14 months. That's why "default alive" is the new mantra.
