## LinkedIn post (paste-ready)

A startup sells for ₹500Cr. The founder can still walk away with ₹0.

I didn't believe this either. Then I read my first term sheet.

The clause is called a liquidation preference. It decides who gets paid first when a company exits — and VCs are always in front of the line.

Think of it like a chit fund where one member wrote the rules: the investor takes their money back FIRST, at 1x or 2x, before founders and employees see a rupee.

₹500Cr exit − ₹300Cr raised with 1.5x preference = ₹450Cr to investors. ₹50Cr left for everyone who built the thing.

The headline says "₹500Cr exit." The term sheet says who actually won.

What clause should I tear apart next?

3. THE NUMBER — **1x–2x** — the multiple investors take back before anyone else gets paid.
4. WHY IT MATTERS — Headlines report exit values. Term sheets decide outcomes. These are different documents.
5. MECHANISM — Chit fund analogy: one member wrote the rules. Investor money returns first, multiplied. What's left is shared.
6. VC LENS — It's downside protection. VCs lose on 7 of 10 bets — preferences make the losses survivable.
7. MY TAKE — I thought valuation was the deal. The preference stack IS the deal. Nobody teaches this in B-school.
8. CLOSE — "Would you sign a 2x preference to get funded?" + brand bar.
